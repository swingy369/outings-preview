# Seven-Day Behaviour Diary

**Use with:** `README.md`, `04-consent-and-privacy.md`, `01-assumption-evidence-map.md`, and `evidence-matrix.csv`  
**Session type for evidence:** `diary`  
**Purpose:** Capture ordinary, recent meal decisions as they happen, including routines that work well. This is not a food, health, safety, or location-monitoring record.

## Researcher setup — before giving the diary

1. Assign and write only the analytical research ID: `WA-###`. Do not put the participant's name, contact details, precise address, diagnosis, or detailed health history in this diary.
2. Confirm `participation_consent=yes` and `diary_consent=yes` in the participant tracker. Diary participation is separate from recording, anonymised quotations, and future contact. Use the canonical state labels `yes`, `no`, `withdrawn`, or `not_asked`; do not infer consent from a completed page.
3. Give or read the participant information sheet in `04-consent-and-privacy.md`. Remind the participant that they may skip any prompt, leave a page blank, pause, stop, or later withdraw using the approved contact process. The diary is research, not a service, clinical assessment, care plan, or monitoring arrangement.
4. Ask the participant which low-burden format they prefer: printed pages completed by hand, one printed sheet per day, a simple digital form or note, or brief dictated answers recorded by the researcher only if separately agreed. The participant may use short words, ticks, or a few sentences; complete sentences and all seven days are not required.
5. Agree how the diary will be returned without placing contact details in the research file. Keep the contact record separate. Do not ask for continuous updates or check-ins.

### Participant-facing introduction and privacy reminder

> We are interested in how ordinary meal choices happen in real life, including choices that are easy, satisfying, repeated, changed, or did not happen. There are no right meals and no right amount to write. Please record only what you are comfortable sharing. You can skip anything, stop at any time, and leave days blank. We use your research ID rather than your name in the research notes. This is not a health, safety, or food-monitoring diary.

### Do not collect

Do not collect calories, “healthy” ratings, location history, or photographic proof. Do not ask for a photograph; no photograph is required. Do not ask the participant to prove that a meal happened, report weight or medical information, or account for every meal or every day.

## Missed-day guidance

Missing a day, a meal, or part of a page is acceptable. Do not try to reconstruct it unless you want to. A gap is **not evidence of risk**, a problem, a missed meal, or a lack of interest. Continue with the next day, write “nothing to add,” or return only the pages you choose.

## Researcher diary review and evidence capture

Review only the pages the participant chooses to return. Do not interpret silence or an omitted page as an event. Save the material under `WA-###`; keep direct identifiers and operational return details separate.

For each discrete diary item, add a separate de-identified row to `evidence-matrix.csv` with `session_type=diary`. Preserve the participant's wording in `verbatim_or_observation`; place the researcher's explanation only in `researcher_interpretation`. Use only the allowed `evidence_type` values: `observed_behaviour`, `reported_behaviour`, `attitude`, `third_party_inference`, or `researcher_interpretation`.

Diary prompts can inform these assumptions without presuming that they are true:

| Assumption IDs | Diary evidence to look for | Evidence that can weaken the assumption |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `P1`, `P2`, `W1` | Participant-owned outcome, repeated decision, trigger, frequency, consequence, workaround | Satisfied routine, rare event, no desired change |
| `P3` | Non-clinical planning, shopping, access, cooking-for-one, motivation, or coordination context | A scope-boundary concern; record minimum necessary detail and do not assess it |
| `U1` | Broad segment contrast recorded elsewhere and a relevant routine context | No meaningful contrast from adjacent routines |
| `B1` | Alternatives used, considered, accepted trade-offs, and sufficiency | An alternative works well enough and resolves the need |
| `R1`, `R2`, `T1` | Invited, ignored, accepted, or rejected help; chosen information boundaries | Help feels controlling, unwanted, or unnecessary; no shared problem |
| `C2` | Past time, money, effort, or trade-off and continuation conditions | No meaningful sacrifice or reason to continue |

Capture a satisfying or no-problem account as carefully as a difficulty. `parent_owned=yes` only when the older adult expresses or demonstrates the outcome as their own. For `recency`, use the diary date or a participant-stated concrete reference; otherwise use `not stated`. Record a plausible `alternative_explanation` and item-specific `contradictory_evidence` rather than resolving contradictions.
