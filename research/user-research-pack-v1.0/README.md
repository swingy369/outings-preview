## Purpose and required research outputs

Use this pack to decide whether a meals-first, non-clinical Weare MVP PRD is justified. The study investigates independently living older adults' meal routines and the role that adult children or other family supporters may play. It tests a problem before proposing a solution.

Before an MVP PRD can be authorised, the research must produce evidence for all five outputs:

1. A specific initial user segment.
2. A recurring triggering situation.
3. A parent-owned job or desired outcome.
4. The user's current behaviour and alternatives.
5. An important unmet need that existing approaches do not adequately resolve.

## Scope and exclusions

**In scope:** independently living older adults in Toronto/GTA; adult children or family supporters; meal decisions, cooking-for-one behaviour, grocery routines, family involvement, current alternatives, and prior attempts to improve the situation. The study uses separate individual interviews, selected paired family interviews, seven-day behaviour diaries, and qualitative synthesis.

**Explicit exclusions:** product feature prioritisation; production-app requirements; prototype usability testing; clinical nutrition, diagnosis, treatment, medication, emergency response, health-risk scoring, or claims about health or extending independence. This pack does not conduct Hong Kong discovery or provide Cantonese or Mandarin materials. It does not validate outings, social connection, or broader independent-living modules, except when participants raise them naturally as context.

## Sample and session targets

- 15 older-adult individual interviews.
- 15 adult-child individual interviews.
- five paired family interviews.
- At least five seven-day behaviour diaries.

These are 30 individual interviews plus five follow-up paired sessions; a paired session never substitutes for either person's individual interview. Recruit purposeful contrasts rather than a statistically representative sample. At least half of older-adult participants should be recruited through channels other than a participating adult child.

Track contrasts including living arrangement, age band, cooking frequency, wish to cook more, smartphone confidence, family distance and contact frequency, help history, cultural meal context, current food alternatives, and whether help was welcomed, ignored, or rejected.

## Research principles

1. Put problem discovery before solution discussion; do not introduce proposed Weare features during core discovery.
2. Treat older-adult evidence as indispensable; adult-child concern alone does not establish an unmet problem.
3. Prefer recent behaviour, workarounds, spending, and trade-offs over opinions or hypothetical enthusiasm.
4. Seek disconfirmation through sceptics, satisfied non-cooks, people who rejected family help, and people whose alternatives work well.
5. Keep perspectives separate before a dyad conversation.
6. Obtain granular consent; participation, recording, diary, quotations, and future contact are separate choices.
7. Collect only the minimum necessary information and never frame research as surveillance, care, or safety monitoring.
8. Keep verbatim or observed evidence separate from the researcher's interpretation.
9. Treat positive and negative evidence with equal care; this research cannot establish product-market fit.

## Fieldwork sequence

1. Recruit against the segmentation matrix and purposeful contrasts.
2. Assign a research ID, record consent choices, and hold contact details separately.
3. Run individual interviews and complete the same-day debrief before the next session.
4. Invite a selected contrast sample to a seven-day behaviour diary.
5. Run a paired family interview only when both people have completed individual interviews.
6. Add atomic evidence to the evidence matrix and separate evidence from interpretation.
7. Run interim synthesis at about one-third and two-thirds of individual interviews; fill evidence gaps without changing questions to pursue a preferred conclusion.
8. Complete final synthesis, apply the evidence rubric, and record a proceed, narrow, reframe, or stop decision.
9. Authorise an MVP PRD only if the hard gate below is met.

## Session-order rule for dyads

For every dyad, interview the older adult and adult child separately first. Conduct the paired interview only after both individual interviews are complete. Do not disclose or pressure either person to disclose content from a private interview, and do not use the paired session to resolve family disagreement.

## Research IDs and consent states

Assign IDs sequentially in the format `WA-###` (for example, `WA-001`). A research ID is analytical, not anonymous: it links study material for analysis. A separately held contact list may still identify a participant. Keep that contact list away from notes, diaries, synthesis, and analytical CSVs.

Use these consent-state labels exactly in the participant tracker:

- `yes` — explicitly agreed.
- `no` — explicitly declined.
- `withdrawn` — permission or participation was withdrawn.
- `not_asked` — the choice has not yet been requested.

## Privacy and incident-handling boundaries

Use research IDs in notes, diary analysis, and synthesis. Store operational contact information separately and do not place names, emails, telephone numbers, precise addresses, diagnoses, detailed health histories, or unrelated family information in the analytical files. Do not collect continuous monitoring data or photographic proof. Recording is optional and requires explicit separate consent; declining recording does not affect participation. Participants may skip any question or withdraw.

Do not interpret a missed diary entry, unshared activity, or silence as danger. This is non-clinical product research, not monitoring, care, a health assessment, or emergency support. If someone raises an immediate safety concern, pause the research role and follow a separately approved escalation policy; this pack supplies no clinical or emergency advice. Do not reuse materials for model training, marketing, or a materially different purpose without separate consent and appropriate review.

## Hard MVP PRD gate

**No MVP PRD may be commissioned, drafted, or authorised until all five required research outputs are supported by the decision rubric:** a specific initial user segment, a recurring triggering situation, a parent-owned job or desired outcome, the user's current behaviour and alternatives, and an important unmet need not adequately resolved by those alternatives. The evidence must include behavioural evidence, recurrence, multiple independent participants, parent ownership, consideration of alternatives, and explicit contradictory cases. The final documented decision must be `proceed`, `narrow`, `reframe`, or `stop`; only `proceed` permits an MVP PRD.
