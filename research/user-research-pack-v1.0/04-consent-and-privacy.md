# Consent and Privacy Materials

**Status:** Requires legal review before field use. This module is plain-language research material, not legal advice. Complete every operational field and obtain appropriate legal review before recruitment or data collection.

**Use with:** `README.md`, `participant-tracker.csv`, `03-screeners-and-outreach.md`, and the relevant session guide. Give the participant information sheet before asking for consent. Record each choice using the README labels: `yes`, `no`, `withdrawn`, or `not_asked`.

## Participant information sheet

### Why am I being asked?

Weare is conducting this research. We are learning about everyday meal decisions, cooking or other meal routines, and the role that adult children or other family supporters may play. We want to understand what people do now, what works well, and what they may want to be different. This is research, not a service.

### What would I do?

If you choose to take part, you may be invited to a research conversation about your everyday meal routines and, if relevant, family support. An individual conversation usually lasts 45–60 minutes. Some people may separately be invited to keep a seven-day diary or to join a paired family discussion after both people have completed their own individual conversations. You can decide separately whether to take part in any diary or paired discussion.

You may skip any question, take a break, ask to move on, or stop the session at any time. You do not need to explain why.

### Are there any possible discomforts?

Some questions about family support, offers of help, or differing views may feel personal or uncomfortable. You may choose not to discuss any topic. In a paired family discussion, either person may pause or end the discussion at any time.

### What this research does not provide

This study is not a clinical service, emergency service, or monitoring service. It does not provide care, diagnosis, treatment, safety assessment, continuous monitoring, or a response service. Taking part does not create a care relationship or an obligation for Weare to monitor you.

### What information will you collect?

We collect only information needed for this study, such as broad details about meal routines, family-support experiences, responses to questions, optional recordings, and optional diary entries. We use a research ID such as `WA-###` on research notes, diaries, recordings, and analysis materials.

Your research ID is not the same as being anonymous: it links your study materials for analysis. Your name and contact details, if needed to schedule or follow up, are kept in a separately stored contact record. The contact record is kept away from notes, diaries, synthesis files, and analytical CSVs. We do not put names, email addresses, telephone numbers, precise addresses, diagnoses, detailed health histories, or unrelated family information in analytical files.

### Who can see the research?

Only the research team and authorised people who need to administer, analyse, or review this study may access the relevant research materials. Access is limited to what each person needs for the study. Contact records are accessed separately from de-identified research materials.

## Granular consent script and record

Record each answer as `yes` or `no` at the time it is asked. Use `not_asked` when the choice has not been presented, and `withdrawn` if a previously given permission is withdrawn. Record the applicable choices in `participant-tracker.csv` without changing its header; use the session consent record, keyed only by research ID, for the conditional video choice.

| Separate choice | Read aloud | Yes / no choice and record |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Study participation | “Do you agree to take part in this research study?” | Yes / No. Record in `participation_consent`. |
| Audio recording | “Do you agree to audio recording of this session?” | Yes / No. Record in `recording_consent`. Declining audio recording does not prevent core interview participation. |
| Video recording, if ever proposed | “Video recording is optional. Do you agree to video recording of this session?” | Yes / No. Seek this only if video is proposed; otherwise record `not_asked` in the session consent record. Declining video recording does not prevent core interview participation. |
| Diary participation | “If you are invited, do you agree to take part in the seven-day meal diary?” | Yes / No. Record in `diary_consent`. A `yes` permits the diary invitation, not any unagreed recording. |
| Anonymised quotations | “May we use short anonymised quotations from what you say in research findings? We will remove direct identifying details where reasonably possible.” | Yes / No. Record in `quotation_preference`. |
| Future research contact | “May we keep your separately stored contact details to ask whether you would like to hear about a future research opportunity?” | Yes / No. Record in `future_contact_consent`. Declining future contact does not prevent core interview participation. |

Before proceeding, say: “Your answers are separate. You can take part in the interview even if you say no to recording or future research contact.” If the person says no to study participation, do not collect research content and update the participation field to `no`.

## Paired-session safeguard

Use a paired session only after both people have completed separate individual interviews and both agree to the paired discussion. Read this reminder at the start:

> Each of you may have shared things in your individual interview. I will not disclose private individual-interview content to the other person. Please share only what you are comfortable discussing together. Either of you can pause, skip a question, or end the paired discussion at any time.

Do not use the paired session to resolve a disagreement or pressure either person to disclose private information.

## Withdrawal and deletion workflow

1. Receive the request, confirm the person’s research ID where possible, and record the request date and `withdrawn` consent state in the relevant tracker fields. Do not record the reason unless the person freely gives a minimal operational reason and it is needed to process the request.
2. Locate only study materials linked to that research ID: notes, diary entries, recordings, quotation selections, and the separately stored contact record where applicable. Keep the contact record separate from analytical files throughout.
3. Stop any future contact or further data collection under that ID immediately. Remove the person from unsent invitations and scheduled sessions.
4. Delete or securely destroy material that can still be linked to that research ID, including separately held contact details unless a different approved retention duty applies. Record that the action was completed in the operational withdrawal log, keyed by research ID.
5. Explain the limitation: material already de-identified so it cannot reasonably be linked to the ID, or already included in aggregate reporting, may not be removable. Do not make a promise to delete material that cannot be identified.

## Sensitive-disclosure and incident safeguards

If someone accidentally shares sensitive information that is outside the study’s minimum necessary data, do not probe for more detail. Acknowledge the choice to skip or pause, record only the minimum research-relevant information needed, and keep unnecessary identifying or sensitive details out of notes and analytical files. If a recording contains unneeded sensitive detail, handle it under the approved data-handling process before analysis or quotation selection.

If an urgent safety concern arises, pause the research. The next steps must use a separately approved escalation policy. This consent module does not provide clinical, legal, hotline, or emergency instructions.
