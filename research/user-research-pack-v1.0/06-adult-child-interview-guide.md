# Adult-Child Individual Interview Guide

**Session:** 45–60 minutes | **Session type:** `adult_child_individual` | **Research ID:** `WA-###`

**Purpose:** Understand the adult child's own actions, concerns, trade-offs, and boundaries around everyday meals and family support. Their account is a separate perspective: it does not establish that the older adult has a problem or wants change. This guide is for adult children in the core target; other family supporters, if included as exploratory cases, remain out of quota and must be recorded as such.

## Facilitator setup (before the participant joins)

- Confirm the `WA-###` ID and the participant's classification. Only an adult child counts toward the 15-person adult-child core target; do not count exploratory supporters as a substitute.
- Confirm the information sheet was provided and keep names, precise places, diagnoses, detailed health histories, and unrelated family information out of analytical notes.
- Review only the participant's own screener context. Do not use or reveal content from an older adult's session, even if the pair may later be invited to a dyad.

## 1. Consent and opening (5–7 minutes)

Use the researcher introduction and granular consent script in `04-consent-and-privacy.md`. Record each separate choice using `yes`, `no`, `withdrawn`, or `not_asked`; participation, recording, diary participation, quotations, and future contact are independent.

**Recording check:** Ask separately for audio recording before starting. A no means notes only and does not end the interview. Ask about video only if it is actually proposed; otherwise record `not_asked` in the session consent record.

Say: “We are learning about everyday meal decisions and family support, including situations where routines are comfortable and no one wants a change. This is research, not a service. You may skip, pause, or stop at any point.”

Rapport questions:

1. “What has been taking up your time lately?”
2. “How would you describe your usual contact with your family member, if you would like to?”
3. “What kinds of everyday practical matters, if any, do you tend to talk about together?”

**Do not lead:** Do not ask the participant to assess the older adult's capability, diagnose a cause, or prove that a concern is justified.

## 2. Recent event that prompted concern — or Recent evidence of no concern (10–12 minutes)

“Please take me back to the last specific event about meals or food that made you concerned, if there was one. If not, tell me about a recent event that showed you things were fine or that it was not your concern.”

Build a concrete timeline: when it happened, what the adult child directly saw/heard/did, what they did next, who else was involved, and what happened afterward.

Ask two columns aloud and document them separately:

| Directly observed or personally done | Assumption, report from someone else, or interpretation |
| --- | --- |
| “What did you directly see, hear, pay for, arrange, or do?” | “What are you inferring about your parent? What makes that uncertain?” |

**Probes:** “What were the exact words, if you remember?” “When did this occur?” “Was it typical or unusual?” “What else could explain it?” “What did your parent say they wanted, if anything?”

**Do not lead:** Worry is not proof of a parent-owned problem. Record the adult child's account of the older adult as `third_party_inference` unless it is a direct description of the adult child's own behaviour.

## 3. Current support, calls, visits, and alternatives (10–12 minutes)

“What do you currently do, if anything, around meals or food?” Include calls, visits, shopping, ordering, cooking, sharing information, coordinating with others, and choosing not to get involved.

Explore:

- Frequency and triggers for calls, visits, food support, coordination, reminders, or workarounds.
- What the adult child actually does versus what another person or service does.
- Existing meal options and practical alternatives they know of; it is valid not to know.
- What is reliable, enough, burdensome, costly, time-consuming, or neutral for the adult child.

Ask: “What are you doing now that already works?” “What do you deliberately not do?” “What trade-offs do you make, if any?”

## 4. Previous attempts and outcomes (8–10 minutes)

“Tell me about a specific earlier time you tried something different, suggested help, or chose not to act.”

Probe for the initiating event, what was offered or attempted, who chose it, time/money/effort/sacrifice, immediate and later outcome, whether it continued, and why.

Then ask: “Has support ever caused tension or been rejected? What happened, and what did you do next?” Include: “Have you had a situation where your parent preferred no help and that was okay?”

**Do not lead:** Do not describe acceptance of help as success or rejection as a mistake. Do not ask for private health details to explain an interaction.

## 5. Information visibility and relationship boundaries (7–9 minutes)

Use neutral relationship situations, not a proposed solution:

1. “Imagine your parent chooses whether to mention a meal-related difficulty. What information, if any, would be useful for you to know?”
2. “What would be acceptable only if they chose to share it in that moment?”
3. “What would feel unnecessary, intrusive, or likely to create pressure?”
4. “What would you do if they did not want to share?”
5. “What would make support feel like coordination rather than control?”

Listen for useful support or reassurance that does not depend on ongoing access, tracking, proof, alerts, or continuous updates. Treat any wish for those things as evidence to record, not a request to solve.

## 6. Disconfirmation and necessity check (5–7 minutes)

Ask all questions below, including when earlier concern sounded strong:

1. “How do you know this concern is recurring rather than a one-off?”
2. “What would show that it is not important to your parent, or that it is not your role to address?”
3. “What current routine, person, or service could make anything new unnecessary?”
4. “If your parent is satisfied with cooking less or using alternatives, what would you want to respect?”
5. “What evidence would change your mind about whether there is a concern?”

## 7. Close: value and continuation (4–6 minutes)

“Considering only the actions and workarounds you use today, which are worth continuing and which are not?”

Ask: “What makes that worthwhile or not worthwhile to you?” “What time, money, effort, or other sacrifice have you already made, if any?” “What would make you stop or reduce your involvement?”

Do not introduce a price, ask for a hypothetical purchase, or position any offering as necessary.

Close: “What have I missed about your own experience of meals and family support?” Thank the participant, confirm any separately chosen diary or future-contact preference, and remind them of the withdrawal process.

## Facilitator evidence notes

| Assumption IDs | Listen for | Evidence type(s) most likely | Disconfirming evidence to preserve |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `P1`, `P2` | Adult child's direct actions; claims about recurrence; older-adult views reported second-hand | `reported_behaviour`, `third_party_inference` | No recurring concern, older adult reportedly satisfied, one-off event |
| `P3` | Non-clinical practical context in the adult child's own actions | `reported_behaviour`, `third_party_inference` | Main issue outside non-clinical research scope; minimum necessary note only |
| `U1`, `W1` | Meal-routine context, frequency, and contrasts | `reported_behaviour`, `third_party_inference` | No repeated routine issue or no segment distinction |
| `B1` | Current meal alternatives and workarounds; their limits or sufficiency | `reported_behaviour`, `third_party_inference` | Current alternatives work well enough |
| `R1`, `R2` | Invited help, rejection, tension, agreement, mismatch, and role boundaries | `reported_behaviour`, `attitude`, `third_party_inference` | Help creates pressure, no alignment, or no parent-owned need |
| `T1`, `C1` | Acceptable/unacceptable visibility and value without ongoing access | `attitude`, `reported_behaviour` | Value requires tracking, proof, alerts, or access the older adult may not control |
| `C2` | Existing spending, time, effort, sacrifices, and continuation conditions | `reported_behaviour` | No meaningful past sacrifice or reason to continue |

## Post-session Debrief and evidence capture (same day, 8–10 minutes)

1. Check consent and recording states. Save material under `WA-###` only; keep contact details separate.
2. Split every consequential item into direct observation, the adult child's own behaviour, second-hand account/inference about the older adult, and researcher interpretation. Do not collapse them.
3. Add atomic evidence items with `session_type=adult_child_individual`, relevant assumption IDs, recency, current alternative, alternative explanation, contradictory evidence, `source_strength`, `parent_owned`, and `confidence`.
4. Use `third_party_inference` for claims about the older adult that the adult child did not directly observe. `parent_owned=yes` only when the older adult expresses or demonstrates the outcome as their own; adult-child concern alone is not enough.
5. Preserve at least one no-concern, satisfactory-alternative, no-involvement, or rejection account when discussed. Do not use this individual session to determine dyad eligibility or disclose anything to the other participant.

**Self-check:** Did I treat worry as proof, treat a rejected offer as a failure, blur inference with observation, or invite solution discussion? If yes, correct the notes and flag the limitation.
