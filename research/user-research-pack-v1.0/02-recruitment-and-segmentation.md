# Recruitment and Segmentation

**Status:** Field-ready recruitment guidance; not a population-representative sampling plan.

Use this guide with the sample targets, `WA-###` research IDs, dyad order, and PRD gate in `README.md`; use the fields already defined in `participant-tracker.csv`. This study is purposeful qualitative research. It seeks useful contrasts and disconfirming cases, not demographic representativeness or prevalence estimates.

## Recruitment purpose and neutral framing

Recruit people to discuss everyday meal decisions and, where relevant, family support. Do not describe a proposed product, imply that a participant has a meal problem, or suggest that participation will improve anyone's health, safety, independence, or family relationship.

Invite people who would like something to be different **and** people who are satisfied with how they eat now. Evidence that an existing routine or alternative works well is as valuable as evidence of difficulty. Link recruitment choices to the assumptions that need contrast: `P1`, `P2`, `P3`, `U1`, `B1`, `R1`, `R2`, `T1`, `C1`, `C2`, and `W1` in `01-assumption-evidence-map.md`.

## Eligibility

### Older-adult individual interview

Include an older adult who:

- is age 65 or older;
- lives independently in Toronto or the Greater Toronto Area (alone, with a partner, family member, or a roommate, rather than in a setting with continuous supervision);
- can provide their own informed consent and take part in an English conversation; and
- is willing to discuss ordinary meal choices, including a response that their current routine is satisfactory.

### Adult-child individual interview (core target) and other family supporters (exploratory)

Include an adult (18 or older) who:

- is an adult child or other family supporter of an older adult who lives independently in Toronto/GTA;
- is an adult child or another family supporter who has some awareness of, contact about, or a clear lack of involvement in that older adult's everyday meal decisions; and
- can provide their own informed consent and take part in an English conversation.

The core target is **15 in-scope adult-child individual interviews**. Only a participant who identifies as the adult child of an independently living older adult in Toronto/GTA counts toward that 15-person target. An adult child may live outside Toronto/GTA and need not think there is a problem, be enthusiastic about support, or be paired with a participating older adult.

A clear “no” to either the older adult's independent-living status or Toronto/GTA location is out of scope for this study and excludes the person from both the core adult-child sample and exploratory supporter interviews. An “unclear” answer remains an operational screening case only: hold it out of quota and do not schedule or count an interview until both points are minimally confirmed. Use only the high-level checks in `03-screeners-and-outreach.md`; do not seek medical details, a precise address, or an explanation for uncertainty. This rule preserves the hard target of 15 in-scope adult children.

Other family supporters may be recruited only as exploratory, out-of-quota contrast cases. Record them separately from adult children and do **not** count them toward the 15 adult-child interviews, the core sample balance, or a claim that the adult-child target has been met. Uninvolved, sceptical, and satisfied adult children remain important core contrasts; exploratory supporters do not replace them.

### First-study exclusions

Apply these exclusions only to keep this first, non-clinical study within scope. Ask at a high level and do not collect diagnoses or detailed health histories.

- A person currently needs continuous supervision to participate safely in daily life.
- A person has advanced cognitive impairment that prevents informed consent or a meaningful research conversation.
- Meal choices are primarily governed by a medically prescribed nutrition plan that would require clinical assessment to discuss appropriately.
- A current situation requires emergency response or urgent care rather than a research conversation.
- The person cannot provide their own informed consent.
- The person cannot participate in English for this English-language study.

A person may decline to answer a screening question. Do not infer an exclusion from age, disability, use of food delivery, living arrangement, or low smartphone confidence alone. If an urgent concern arises, end recruitment respectfully and follow the separately approved escalation policy; do not collect further detail for this study.

## Purposeful sample and soft quotas

Recruit toward the README targets: 15 older-adult individual interviews, **15 adult-child individual interviews**, five paired family interviews, and at least five seven-day diaries. Other family supporters are exploratory/out-of-quota only and do not count toward the 15 adult-child target. Each paired interview is a follow-up, not a substitute for either individual interview.

At least **eight independently recruited older adults** are required. Mark `recruited_independently` in the tracker for an older adult contacted through a channel not initiated by a participating adult child. This meets the README requirement that at least half of the 15 older-adult participants come through channels other than a participating adult child.

Recruit **five dyads** that are eligible for two separate individual sessions followed by one paired session. Hold the paired session only after both individual interviews are complete and both people separately agree; never use it to resolve disagreement or disclose private interview content.

Use the following as soft quotas. They are prompts for contrast, not exclusion rules or numeric claims about Toronto/GTA.

| Contrast to cover | Soft target across the relevant individual sample | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Living arrangement | Older adults living alone; with partner/spouse; and with family or another adult | Tests whether routines and support differ by household context. |
| Cooking frequency | Rare/non-cooks, occasional cooks, and frequent cooks | Tests `W1` without assuming cooking is desired. |
| Desire to cook more | People who want to cook more, want to cook less, and those who say “I am satisfied with how I eat now” | Directly seeks evidence for and against `P1`. |
| Age band | Coverage across 65–74, 75–84, and 85+ where feasible | Avoids treating older adults as one segment. |
| Smartphone confidence | Low, mixed, and high confidence | Tests whether digital confidence is relevant without making it a requirement. |
| Family distance and contact | Same household/nearby, farther away, and low/high contact | Creates contrast for `R1`, `R2`, `T1`, and `C1`. |
| Family-involvement intensity | `help_history` values with `intensity=none`, `intensity=occasional`, and `intensity=frequent` (`intensity=unknown` when skipped or unclear) | Includes both invited and limited family roles without conflating them with the response to help. |
| Cultural meal context | Self-described cultural, religious, household, and food-preference contexts; include more than one context where recruitment permits | Looks for context-specific routines without assigning identity labels. |
| Current food alternatives | Home cooking, ready-made or delivered meals, restaurants/takeout, family help, community meals, grocery convenience options, and mixed routines | Tests `B1` against viable alternatives. |
| Prior help response | `help_history` values with `response=welcomed`, `response=not_taken_up`, `response=rejected`, `response=mixed`, and `response=not_offered` | Seeks possible disconfirmation of `R1` and `R2` separately from involvement intensity. |

Deliberately include all of the following, even when they express little interest in change:

- satisfied non-cooks;
- people using successful alternatives who do not see an unmet need; and
- older adults who reject family assistance, along with family supporters whose help was not wanted.

Do not replace these cases with more enthusiastic participants. A pattern of satisfaction, successful alternatives, or rejected help may weaken or disprove an assumption and is a required research output.

## Toronto/GTA recruitment channels

Use several channels and record the specific `recruitment_source` in the tracker. Start with channels likely to reach older adults directly as well as family supporters.

- Toronto Public Library branches, seniors' programming, community bulletin boards, and neighbourhood newsletters.
- City of Toronto older-adult, recreation, and community-program contacts that permit neutral research notices.
- Community centres, seniors' centres, cultural associations, faith communities, and tenant or condominium associations across Toronto and nearby GTA municipalities.
- Independent-living apartment buildings and non-care retirement or housing communities, only with site permission and without staff selecting only people they view as needing help.
- Grocery, market, cooking, and social clubs; meal programs; and community food organisations, using a notice that welcomes satisfied routines as well as changes.
- University, hospital, or community-research volunteer registries where their rules allow this non-clinical study.
- Adult-child channels such as caregiver/family networks, local parent groups, workplaces, and community associations, using a separate neutral invitation.
- Paid or community research panels that can filter for Toronto/GTA, when they allow direct screening and do not force a problem-focused profile.

Founder, investor, staff, friend, and family networks may distribute the same public notice only as a small supplementary channel. They must not supply the whole sample, pre-select people thought likely to agree, recruit only enthusiastic adult children, or pressure anyone already known to them.

## Sampling controls and gap reviews

Before each invitation, assign the person a provisional `WA-###` ID only after they agree to be screened. Keep direct contact details in the separate contact record specified by `contact_record_location`; keep only eligibility and segmentation values in the tracker.

### Sampling-gap worksheet (separate structured record)

Keep a de-identified sampling-gap worksheet beside the tracker. This worksheet does not replace or modify the exact `participant-tracker.csv` header. It records only the fields needed to monitor contrasts that the tracker cannot represent as a distinct field:

| Worksheet field | Allowed value or recording rule |
| --- | --- |
| `research_id` | `WA-###` only; no name or contact detail. |
| `participant_type_detail` | `older_adult`, `adult_child_core`, or `other_supporter_exploratory`. Count only `adult_child_core` toward the 15 adult-child target. |
| `cultural_meal_context_code` | `self_described_food_tradition`, `religious_or_observance`, `household_preference_or_restriction`, `none_named`, `prefer_not_to_say`, or `not_asked`. |
| `cultural_context_short_note` | Optional, non-identifying broad meal-context wording volunteered by the participant; never record ethnicity, religion, address, or a detailed personal history. |

Use the optional cultural meal-context screener question in `03-screeners-and-outreach.md` to populate this worksheet. The worksheet and all screening segment values are operational sampling tools, not analytical evidence and not a substitute for participant consent. Keep them operational until, after participation consent, the participant re-confirms the minimum tags needed for analysis under `09-session-notes-and-debrief.md`; only those re-confirmed tags may enter the debrief and evidence matrix.

Review the tracker after approximately **ten** and **twenty** completed individual interviews. At each review:

1. Count older-adult interviews, `adult_child_core` interviews, and `other_supporter_exploratory` interviews separately; do not count a paired session as an individual interview. Only `adult_child_core` interviews count toward the hard 15-person adult-child target.
2. Check that eight or more older adults are on track to be independently recruited and that five potential dyads can complete both individual sessions first.
3. Mark overrepresented and missing values in every tracker contrast field: `living_arrangement`, `cooking_frequency_band`, `wants_to_cook_more`, `age_band`, `smartphone_confidence`, `family_distance_band`, `family_contact_frequency`, `current_food_alternatives`, and both components of `help_history`: `intensity` and `response`.
4. Review the sampling-gap worksheet's `cultural_meal_context_code` field as its own contrast. Adjust distribution channels if a relevant self-described meal context is missing, while retaining `none_named`, `prefer_not_to_say`, and `not_asked` as valid non-problem responses.
5. Check for the deliberate disconfirming cases: satisfied non-cooks, successful alternatives, and `response=rejected` family assistance.
6. Compare recruitment coverage with the assumptions map. For example, missing successful alternatives is a `B1` gap; missing independent older-adult voices is a `P1`, `P2`, and `U1` gap; missing rejected help is an `R1` and `R2` gap.
7. Change channels or invitation distribution to fill the largest gap; do not change questions or relax exclusions to pursue a preferred conclusion.

Record the rationale for any recruitment adjustment in the fieldwork log, not in the analytical tracker. Do not claim that the resulting sample represents the Toronto/GTA older-adult population.

## Anti-bias instructions for recruiters

- Recruit older adults directly, not only through participating adult children, caregivers, or people who already agree with a founder.
- Never screen for interest in a service, willingness to use technology, a meal deficit, family worry, or a desire for change.
- Do not tell an adult child that their older relative has been invited, is eligible, or said anything in screening unless the older adult separately chooses to share that information.
- Invite people with routine satisfaction, low family contact, and low technology confidence with the same respect as people who want change.
- Record declined invitations only as an aggregate operational count where needed; do not create research profiles for people who decline.
- Treat an organisation's referral as a distribution channel, not an endorsement of a participant's need. Ask organisations to share the notice broadly rather than nominate only “good candidates.”
