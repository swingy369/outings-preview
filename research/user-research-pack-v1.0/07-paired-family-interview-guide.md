# Paired Family Follow-up Interview Guide

**Session:** 60 minutes | **Session type:** `paired` | **Dyad ID:** `DY-###` | **Research IDs:** `WA-###` and `WA-###`

**Purpose:** Compare voluntarily shared accounts of one recent meal or support event, identify alignment and mismatch, and understand mutually acceptable boundaries. This is a follow-up after separate individual interviews. It is not a conflict-resolution session and must never disclose private individual-interview content.

## Facilitator setup (before either participant joins)

- Confirm both individual interviews occurred and each person separately agreed to consider and attend this paired discussion. If either condition is not met, do not proceed.
- Confirm the paired-session consent record is ready for each participant. Do not bring individual notes into the room as prompts, and do not name, paraphrase, compare, or hint at what either person said privately.
- Prepare a neutral shared note template using the dyad ID and no direct identifiers. Keep individual evidence linked to its original `WA-###` record; paired evidence must not overwrite it.
- Decide how either person can privately signal a pause or exit. If tension rises, pause, remind both people they can skip or leave, and do not mediate, judge, or seek agreement.

## 1. Consent, boundaries, and ground rules (8–9 minutes)

For each person, renew study participation and recording consent separately under `04-consent-and-privacy.md`. Record each choice as `yes`, `no`, `withdrawn`, or `not_asked`. A refusal of recording means notes only and does not bar participation.

Read the paired-session safeguard:

> Each of you may have shared things in your individual interview. I will not disclose private individual-interview content to the other person. Please share only what you are comfortable discussing together. Either of you can pause, skip a question, or end the paired discussion at any time.

Add: “I am here to understand experiences, not to decide who is right or to settle a disagreement. You may describe different views without resolving them.”

**Recording check:** Ask separately before recording. Ask about video only if it has actually been proposed; otherwise record `not_asked` in the session consent record.

Ground rules:

- One person speaks at a time; each may pass.
- Speak from personal experience (“I noticed…” or “I preferred…”), not for the other person.
- Differences are useful research evidence; neither person has to agree or reveal anything discussed privately.

## 2. Choose one Recent shared event (8–9 minutes)

Say: “Please choose one recent meal decision or practical support event that you are both comfortable discussing today. It can be something ordinary, something that went well, something awkward, or an event that showed no help was needed.”

If they cannot agree on an event, offer neutral choices: a meal that was cooked, bought, received, repeated, skipped, planned, or discussed. If neither person wants to choose, move to a general discussion; do not draw on their individual interviews.

**Do not lead:** Do not select an event based on private knowledge, call it a problem, or imply that cooking, accepting help, or sharing information is the desired outcome.

## 3. Two turns: same event, separate accounts (14–15 minutes)

Give equal uninterrupted turns. Ask one person first, then ask the other exactly the same questions. Alternate who starts across sessions where practical.

1. “Please tell the story from your own point of view, from before the event through what happened next.”
2. “What did you personally see, do, decide, or say?”
3. “What mattered to you in that moment?”
4. “What options did you think were available?”
5. “How did the result feel to you?”

After both turns, reflect only publicly stated content: “I heard two accounts of the event. I will not add anything from private conversations.” Ask: “What parts sound similar?” “What parts sound different?” “Are there any assumptions each of you made that the other person did not know?”

Record agreement, mismatch, and unspoken assumptions without choosing a correct version. A mismatch may be meaningful evidence for `R2`; it is not a problem for the facilitator to resolve.

## 4. Current roles, alternatives, and Previous attempts (9–10 minutes)

Ask each person in turn:

- “What happens now around everyday meals or food support, if anything?”
- “Which current routines or alternatives work well enough?”
- “What help is invited, declined, or not needed?”
- “Tell me about a previous time you tried a different arrangement. What happened afterward?”
- “What effort, time, money, coordination, or trade-off does each person take on, if any?”

Surface no-problem evidence: “Are there parts of the current arrangement that neither of you would change?” and “What would make any additional change unnecessary?”

**Do not lead:** Do not turn the discussion into a negotiation about a future arrangement. Do not use one person's account to challenge the other.

## 5. Helpful support, unacceptable pressure, and information boundaries (9–10 minutes)

Use a short, turn-by-turn relationship scenario. For example: “Imagine an adult child notices a change in a familiar meal routine and wants to be helpful. The older adult may or may not want to discuss it.”

Ask each person:

1. “What would a respectful first step look like?”
2. “What help, if any, would feel useful?”
3. “What would feel like pressure, control, or too much involvement?”
4. “What information would you choose to share, and what would you keep private?”
5. “What should happen when one person wants more information or involvement than the other wants to give?”

Then ask: “What, if anything, can you both agree is acceptable?” “Where do your boundaries differ?” “Is there anything that should remain undecided?”

Never show screens, name a proposed product, or ask either person to accept visibility, updates, proof, tracking, or monitoring. Keep the discussion about their relationship and choices.

## 6. Disconfirmation and Close (6–7 minutes)

Ask both participants:

1. “What in this conversation suggests there may be no recurring issue to address?”
2. “Which current alternative or routine is already sufficient?”
3. “What would show that family involvement is not helpful here?”
4. “What would make this kind of shared discussion unnecessary in the future?”

Summarise only content stated in this session, naming both agreement and difference: “I heard [shared point], and I also heard [difference or uncertainty]. Is that an accurate summary? What would either of you correct or add?”

Thank both participants. Reconfirm that their individual interviews remain private, that no action is expected of either person, and that each may use the approved withdrawal process. Do not solicit a joint commitment or a solution preference.

## Private facilitator Debrief (after both participants leave; 8–10 minutes, outside the 60-minute participant session)

Do this only after both have left or the session is otherwise private. Do not discuss one person's private individual-interview content with the other person before, during, or after this debrief.

1. Note whether both consents and the paired-session safeguard were completed; record only the dyad and research IDs required by the approved tracking process.
2. Separate public paired statements from facilitator observations and interpretations. Do not use the paired session to overwrite either person's individual evidence.
3. Add atomic items with `session_type=paired`, relevant assumption IDs, recency, `evidence_type`, `source_strength`, `parent_owned`, `confidence`, alternative explanation, and contradictory evidence.
4. Capture agreement, mismatch, unspoken assumptions, welcomed/declined support, current alternatives, and information boundaries as separate items. Attribute each view to the appropriate research ID where the matrix permits; retain the dyad ID for the shared context.
5. Record any tension, pause, skip, or exit as a fieldwork limitation, with minimum necessary detail. Do not diagnose, decide who is right, or attempt to mediate later.

| Assumption IDs | Paired evidence to capture | Evidence type(s) | Disconfirming evidence to preserve |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `P1`, `P2`, `W1` | Publicly shared recent routine, trigger, and whether a change is parent-owned | `reported_behaviour`, `attitude` | No issue, rare event, satisfied routine |
| `B1` | Alternatives used jointly or separately and their sufficiency | `reported_behaviour` | Current arrangement resolves the need |
| `R1` | Helpful/invited support, choice, pressure, rejection, tension | `reported_behaviour`, `attitude` | Support is controlling, unwanted, or not needed |
| `R2` | Agreement, mismatch, and unspoken assumptions about event, need, roles, and boundaries | `reported_behaviour`, `attitude`, `researcher_interpretation` | Material conflict or no shared intervention basis |
| `T1`, `C1` | Older-adult-controlled sharing and adult-child value without ongoing access | `attitude` | Either person requires access the other does not accept |
| `C2` | Shared or separate effort, time, money, and continuation conditions | `reported_behaviour` | No meaningful sacrifice or shared reason to continue |

**Self-check:** Did I disclose or hint at individual content, pressure disclosure, speak as a mediator, erase disagreement, or introduce a solution? If yes, stop any further dyad follow-up and flag the incident under the approved process.
